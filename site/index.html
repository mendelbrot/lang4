case

word-final vc suffixes attached to nominal stems (noun-op) to declare their semantic or pragmatic role in the clause. because lexical roots have no inherent part of speech, closing a stem with a c1.01 suffix is what turns it into a noun phrase and anchors it to the predicate.

the 26 cases divide into six functional groups:

  • core arguments (fluid-s alignment): nominative (es) for volitional agents and comparees, accusative (om) for patients, themes, non-volitional stative or process subjects, and standards of comparison, experiencer (et) for conscious perceivers and mental or sensory state holders, recipient (os), beneficiary (on), and topic (ok).
  • space, time, and path: locative (in), ablative (ac), allative (an), extent (ik), and perlative (ut).
  • manner, means, accompaniment, and resource: manner and similative (us), instrumental (ur) for reusable tools, comitative (is), abessive and exclusory (un), and consumptive (af) for depletable resources spent or traded away.
  • reason and contingency quartet: causal (ot), purposive (ob), conditional (av), and concessive (ib). these pair directly with discourse pointers (c1.07) to replace nested subclauses.
  • relational and framing roles: essive (oc), perspective (ov), and substitutive (og).
  • discourse and speaker stance: vocative (as), affective (uv) for the speaker's emotional attitude, and assessive (ic) for the speaker's evaluative judgment.
es
nominative case
volitional agent, actor, comparee

volitional agent, actor, or comparee. marks the deliberate initiator of an action or the subject being evaluated in a comparison.

  • agent: activated with transitive (at) verbs. marks the volitional doer acting upon an accusative (om) object.
  • actor: activated with intransitive process (uc) verbs. marks a volitional performer of a self-contained action (e.g. walking, running). contrasts with accusative (om) for non-volitional processes and experiencer (et) for involuntary sensations.
  • comparee: activated with comparative (ak) verbs. marks the entity being measured against the accusative (om) standard.
i gave my friend the book.

accusative case
patient, theme, transferred or perceived object, non-volitional stative/process subject, standard of comparison ('than')

patient, theme, non-volitional intransitive subject, or standard of comparison. serves as the primary undergoer macro-role, with the verb determining the exact sub-meaning.

  • patient: activated with transitive (at) action or change verbs. marks an entity physically impacted, altered, created, or destroyed.
  • theme.transferred: activated with transfer, exchange, or communication verbs (e.g. give, send, buy, sell, trade). marks the item moved, conveyed, or targeted in the exchange, while the receiver takes recipient (os).
  • theme.stimulus: activated with sensory, emotional, or cognitive verbs paired with an experiencer (et). marks the object perceived, felt toward, or thought of.
  • subject.stative: activated with intransitive state (id) verbs. marks the entity holding a quality, identity, or static condition.
  • subject.process: activated with intransitive process (uc) verbs when the subject lacks volition. marks an entity passively undergoing a process or motion (e.g. a river flowing, ice melting, a person falling).
  • standard: activated with comparative (ak) verbs. marks the benchmark ('than' object) against which the nominative (es) comparee is measured.
  • clause.object: activated when attached to a discourse pointer (this-after or that-before) with a speech, perception, or cognition verb. embeds the referenced clause as the direct object.
i broke the cup.

experiencer case
conscious perceiver, feeler, mental, emotional, or sensory state holder

conscious perceiver, feeler, or mental and sensory state holder.

  • perception: activated with sensory verbs (e.g. see, hear, smell, feel) with an accusative (om) stimulus. marks involuntary or receptive perception, contrasting with nominative (es) for deliberate observation (e.g. looking at, listening to).
  • emotion: activated with emotional or cognitive verbs (e.g. fear, love, enjoy, remember). marks the conscious feeler or thinker.
  • sensation: activated with intransitive state (id) or process (uc) verbs describing internal bodily or mental conditions (e.g. cold, tired, hungry). contrasts with accusative (om) to distinguish felt experience ('i am cold') from objective physical state ('the water is cold').

recipient case
dative, receiver of transfer or communication

dative receiver of transfer or communication.

  • transfer: activated with physical transfer or transaction verbs (e.g. give, hand, send, sell). marks the animate or institutional party receiving the accusative (om) theme.
  • communication: activated with speech, signaling, or demonstration verbs (e.g. tell, ask, show). marks the addressee receiving the message or information.

contrasts with allative (an), which marks spatial endpoints or inanimate destinations rather than a receiving party.

i gave my friend the book.

marks a participant who benefits from the clause.

topic case
about, regarding, concerning, subject matter of discourse, thought, or media

aboutness or subject matter ('about, regarding, concerning').

  • discourse: activated with communication, media, or documentation verbs and nouns (e.g. talk, read, write, story). marks the subject matter being discussed or depicted.
  • cognition: activated with mental verbs (e.g. think, wonder, worry, dream). marks the subject occupying the mind, especially when an accusative (om) object is already present or no direct object is taken.

never used for physical containment (use locative in or a compound) or transferred objects (use accusative om).

locative case
at, in, on, during, static location in space or time

static location in space or time ('at, in, on, during').

  • spatial: activated with place, surface, or container nouns. marks static physical position without motion toward or away.
  • temporal: activated with time nouns (e.g. day, night, noon, winter). marks the point or window in time when the event occurs.
  • discourse.concurrent: activated when attached to a discourse pointer (that-before). links two clauses as simultaneous ('meanwhile, at that same time').

allative case
to, toward, destination, until, into, endpoint in space, time, state, or exchange gain

endpoint in space, time, state, or exchange ('to, toward, destination, until, into').

  • spatial.destination: activated with motion or placement verbs with place nouns. marks the physical target or arrival point.
  • spatial.direction: activated with orientation or aimed verbs (e.g. face, shout, swing). marks direction without implying physical arrival or impact.
  • temporal.end: activated with time nouns. marks the ending boundary in time ('until, up to').
  • state.result: activated with change or transformation verbs (e.g. melt, freeze, turn). marks the resulting state or form ('into').
  • exchange.gain: activated with outgoing exchange verbs (e.g. sell, give, convert) where the accusative (om) is the item parted with. marks the money, item, or value gained in return ('sold for five coins').
  • discourse.endpoint: activated when attached to a discourse pointer (that-before). means 'until then, up to that point'.

extent case
over, for the span of, spatial extent or temporal duration

spatial extent or temporal duration ('over, across, for the span of').

  • spatial: activated with distance, length, or area nouns. marks the physical span covered by an action or state (e.g. walking five kilometers).
  • temporal: activated with duration nouns (e.g. hours, years, a moment). marks how long an action or state continues.

contrasts with locative (in), which locates an event at a point or frame rather than measuring its span.

path, route, or conduit traversed ('along, through, via').

  • spatial.path: activated with motion verbs with path, opening, or medium nouns (e.g. road, forest, tunnel, air). marks the route or medium moved through.
  • channel: activated with communication or transfer verbs with conduit nouns (e.g. mail, radio, messenger). marks the channel through which something is sent.

manner case
-ly, in the way of, how an action is performed, like, resembling (similative)

how an action is performed, including similative resemblance ('-ly, in the way of, like, resembling').

  • quality: activated with quality, state, or abstract action nouns. modifies how the verb is carried out (e.g. quickly, gently, carefully).
  • similative: activated with concrete entity, creature, or role nouns (e.g. deer, child, doctor). expresses analogy or likeness ('like a deer, in the manner of a doctor').

contrasts with essive (oc), which marks actual held status rather than mere resemblance.

instrumental case
using, by means of, with, reusable tool or instrument

reusable instrument, tool, or means ('using, by means of, with').

  • tool: activated with action verbs with concrete object nouns. marks a physical implement or device that is used without being consumed by the action.
  • means: activated with action verbs with abstract method, skill, or language nouns. marks a non-depletable technique or medium.

contrasts with consumptive (af), which marks depletable fuel, ingredients, or resources used up during the action.

companion or co-participant ('accompanying, together with').

  • companion: activated with animate nouns. marks someone participating alongside another argument in the clause.
  • accompaniment: activated with inanimate nouns. marks an item carried, worn, or present alongside without serving as a tool (e.g. arriving with a bag, tea with milk).

abessive case
without, lacking, absent, except for, excluding (exclusory)

absence, lack, or exclusion ('without, lacking, except for, excluding').

  • absence: activated with any verb and a missing participant, tool, or feature ('without a map, lacking salt').
  • exclusory: activated in clauses containing universal or group quantifiers (e.g. all, everyone, every day). marks the member excluded from the set ('except for sam, excluding sunday').

consumptive case
consuming, expending, on, in exchange for, depletable resource used up, spent, or traded away

depletable resource used up, spent, or traded away ('consuming, expending, on, in exchange for'). strictly one-way; always marks what is surrendered or depleted.

  • fuel: activated with machine, process, or biological verbs. marks power or fuel burned to run the action ('runs on gas, runs on three hours of sleep').
  • material: activated with crafting, cooking, or building verbs. marks raw ingredients or single-use supplies consumed into the result ('baked bread using flour, painted using two cans').
  • expenditure: activated with effortful verbs with time, energy, or life nouns. marks finite personal or collective resources spent ('wrote it spending two hours, won at the cost of ten lives').
  • trade.away: activated with incoming or symmetric exchange verbs (e.g. buy, take, rent, trade). marks the price or item surrendered in exchange ('bought bread for five coins', 'traded a coat for a hat'). contrasts with allative (an), which marks the item gained when selling.
  • sustenance: activated with survival or feeding verbs. marks what an organism lives on ('survives on leaves').

causal case
because of, caused by, therefore, realized cause or reason

realized cause or reason ('because of, caused by, therefore'). reason quartet 1/4.

  • cause: activated with event, force, or state nouns. marks an actual, realized fact that brought about the clause.
  • reciprocity: activated with social or legal response verbs (e.g. thank, reward, fine, punish). marks the prior deed that prompted the response ('thanked her for her help').
  • discourse.consequence: activated when attached to a discourse pointer (that-before). links clauses by cause and effect ('because of that, therefore, so').

purposive case
for the goal of, in order to, intended future motive or goal

intended future motive or goal ('for the goal of, in order to'). reason quartet 2/4.

  • goal: activated with volitional action verbs with target nouns. marks a desired, unrealized outcome motivating the action.
  • discourse.purpose: activated when attached to a discourse pointer (that-before or this-after). links clauses by intention ('for that purpose, in order to').

contrasts with causal (ot), which marks a realized cause rather than an unrealized future goal.

hypothetical or uncertain condition ('given, in the event of, if'). reason quartet 3/4.

  • condition: activated with event or state nouns. marks a hypothetical circumstance under which the clause holds ('in case of rain, given enough time').
  • discourse.condition: activated when attached to a discourse pointer (that-before or this-after). links clauses conditionally ('if that is the case, then, on condition that').

obstacle overcome or contrast ('despite, in spite of, although, however'). reason quartet 4/4.

  • obstacle: activated with event, force, or state nouns. marks a real or hypothetical barrier that fails to block the clause ('despite the rain, in spite of fear').
  • discourse.contrast: activated when attached to a discourse pointer (that-before). links clauses by contrast ('however, but, despite that, nevertheless').

role, status, or capacity held during the clause ('as, in the capacity of').

  • capacity: activated with profession, role, or function nouns. marks an actual status or office held while performing the action ('works as a doctor, spoke as a leader').
  • stage: activated with life-stage or temporary identity nouns. marks the phase or status during which the clause holds ('as a child, while a student').

contrasts with manner (us), which only expresses resemblance rather than actual status.

valency

word-final vc suffixes attached to predicate stems (verb-op) to turn any stem into a clause verb and specify how many core arguments it takes and how action flows between them.

rather than relying on a standalone copula, c1.02 distinguishes intransitive processes (uc), intransitive states or classifications (id), transitive or causative actions on an accusative object (at), and comparative predicates (ak), which pair an es comparee against an om standard using a c1.10 degree marker on the parameter stem. reflexive (ul) and reciprocal (uk) voices handle self-directed and mutual actions.

tense-aspect

optional cv suffixes (verb-op, or stem-internal for compositional participles) that locate an event in time and describe its internal temporal shape.

the category combines two complementary temporal axes:

  • tense (rank 1): past (gu), present (nu), and future (ye), anchoring the event relative to reference or speech time.
  • aspect (rank 2): perfective (fu) for bounded wholes, imperfective (wu) for open-ended situations, progressive (ma) for actions actively underway at reference time, and habitual (je) for regular routines.

unmarked clauses leave tense and aspect open to context.

modality

optional cv suffixes (verb-op, or stem-internal for compositional participles) expressing volition, capability, obligation, possibility, or hypothetical framing toward an event.

the nine modalities span three semantic domains:

  • volition and dynamic capability: desire (fe) and ability (di).
  • deontic force: necessity (wo), social or moral obligation (bo), advisability (se), and permission (da).
  • epistemic and irrealis framing: prediction (li), possibility (ni), and hypothetical or counterfactual (na).

evidential

optional cv suffixes (verb-op) declaring how the speaker knows the information asserted in the clause. unmarked clauses omit source specification.

the seven evidentials divide epistemic territory into three branches:

  • firsthand knowledge: direct sensory observation (ta) in the present or recent past, older or fallible personal memory (ha), and experiential or lived-through internal knowledge (su).
  • indirect knowledge: inference from observable clues (ci) and tacit intuition or felt sense (sa).
  • secondhand knowledge: informal spoken report or hearsay (jo) and trusted expertise, reputable sources, or established common knowledge (yo).

evidentials strictly encode information source; emotional reaction and evaluative skepticism are kept orthogonal in c1.01 (uv affective and ic assessive).

mood

cv markers that govern the illocutionary force of a clause or manage live conversational turn-taking. declarative statements are unmarked.

the eight elements split into two syntactic behaviors:

  • clause-attaching speech acts: imperative and hortative (zi), prohibitive (du), polar yes/no question (le), and content question (zo, pairing with the interrogative pronoun wa in c1.07).
  • standalone conversational interactionals: affirmative (ji), negative (pu), social greeting or closing (ki), and compliant acceptance (to).

reference

cv nominal stems that track participants, anchor deixis, form questions, and link flat paratactic clauses without subclause nesting. because they function as nominal stems, they take c1.01 case suffixes directly.

the eleven reference words cover four functions:

  • personal pronouns: clusivity-marking first person exclusive (mi) and inclusive (ze), second person (yu), and proximate (ke) vs. obviate (mo) third-person tracking.
  • variable and generic pronouns: interrogative (wa) and indefinite (va).
  • deixis: a compositional demonstrative base (lo) that combines with lexical roots for proximal, distal, person-anchored, or multi-dimensional pointing.
  • discourse pointers: anaphoric (ju), cataphoric (mu), and pre-anaphoric (ka), which point to adjacent clauses and take c1.01 cases to express complex inter-clausal relations.

quantity

cv numerals, quantifiers, and set-focus postfixes used for exact counting, mathematical expression, and scoping over sets. bare nouns carry general, number-neutral reference; c1.08 markers attach only when count or set scope is needed.

the twelve elements cover three related systems:

  • base-6 (senary) numerals: digits zero through five (pa, we, tu, gi, so, lu) and the positional base-6 multiplier operator (fi, representing *6^).
  • set quantifiers: existential (cu), universal (ce), and distributive (ru).
  • scalar and set focus: exclusive focus (ca, restricting to a sole referent) and additive focus (g08.12, adding a referent to an existing set or expectation).

connector

binary cv infixes (x-op-y) placed between two stems to define how the right-hand stem (y) modifies, coordinates with, blends into, or possesses the left-hand head (x).

the eleven connectors divide into two groups:

  • general compounding and coordination: the compound head-modifier divider (pi), symmetric coordination (ge), symmetric concept blending (ve), and the tail-specified general connector (bi), whose exact relation is defined by a single lexical root immediately following it (such as boolean logical connectives).

degree

derivation

unary cv postfixes (x-op) attached inside stems to shift an entity's phase, configuration, participant focus, or metalinguistic framing.

the ten elements cover four derivational operations:

  • phase and outcome: completive goal-reached outcome (do), inceptive beginning or nascent stage (vu), and terminative ending or residual stage (de).
  • configuration: proximal portion or slice of a whole (bu) and coalescent collective or unified group (yi).
  • participant conversion: converting a process stem to refer to its patient or target (vo) or its domain-neutral agent or doer (be).
  • framing and essence: proper name or loanword marker (ya), representative essence for fictional, imagined, or depicted entities (ra), and explicit within-domain figurative metaphor (ko).
