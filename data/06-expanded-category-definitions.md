# c1.01
## case

word-final vc suffixes attached to nominal stems (`noun-op`) to declare their semantic or pragmatic role in the clause. because lexical roots have no inherent part of speech, closing a stem with a `c1.01` suffix is what turns it into a noun phrase and anchors it to the predicate.

the 26 cases divide into six functional groups:

- core arguments (fluid-s alignment): nominative (`es`) for volitional agents and comparees, accusative (`om`) for patients, themes, non-volitional stative or process subjects, and standards of comparison, experiencer (`et`) for conscious perceivers and mental or sensory state holders, recipient (`os`), beneficiary (`on`), and topic (`ok`).
- space, time, and path: locative (`in`), ablative (`ac`), allative (`an`), extent (`ik`), and perlative (`ut`).
- manner, means, accompaniment, and resource: manner and similative (`us`), instrumental (`ur`) for reusable tools, comitative (`is`), abessive and exclusory (`un`), and consumptive (`af`) for depletable resources spent or traded away.
- reason and contingency quartet: causal (`ot`), purposive (`ob`), conditional (`av`), and concessive (`ib`). these pair directly with discourse pointers (`c1.07`) to replace nested subclauses.
- relational and framing roles: essive (`oc`), perspective (`ov`), and substitutive (`og`).
- discourse and speaker stance: vocative (`as`), affective (`uv`) for the speaker's emotional attitude, and assessive (`ic`) for the speaker's evaluative judgment.

# c1.02
## valency

word-final vc suffixes attached to predicate stems (`verb-op`) to turn any stem into a clause verb and specify how many core arguments it takes and how action flows between them.

rather than relying on a standalone copula, `c1.02` distinguishes intransitive processes (`uc`), intransitive states or classifications (`id`), transitive or causative actions on an accusative object (`at`), and comparative predicates (`ak`), which pair an `es` comparee against an `om` standard using a `c1.10` degree marker on the parameter stem. reflexive (`ul`) and reciprocal (`uk`) voices handle self-directed and mutual actions.

# c1.03
## tense-aspect

optional cv suffixes (`verb-op`, or stem-internal for compositional participles) that locate an event in time and describe its internal temporal shape.

the category combines two complementary temporal axes:

- tense (rank 1): past (`gu`), present (`nu`), and future (`ye`), anchoring the event relative to reference or speech time.
- aspect (rank 2): perfective (`fu`) for bounded wholes, imperfective (`wu`) for open-ended situations, progressive (`ma`) for actions actively underway at reference time, and habitual (`je`) for regular routines.

unmarked clauses leave tense and aspect open to context.

# c1.04
## modality

optional cv suffixes (`verb-op`, or stem-internal for compositional participles) expressing volition, capability, obligation, possibility, or hypothetical framing toward an event.

the nine modalities span three semantic domains:

- volition and dynamic capability: desire (`fe`) and ability (`di`).
- deontic force: necessity (`wo`), social or moral obligation (`bo`), advisability (`se`), and permission (`da`).
- epistemic and irrealis framing: prediction (`li`), possibility (`ni`), and hypothetical or counterfactual (`na`).

# c1.05
## evidential

optional cv suffixes (`verb-op`) declaring how the speaker knows the information asserted in the clause. unmarked clauses omit source specification.

the seven evidentials divide epistemic territory into three branches:

- firsthand knowledge: direct sensory observation (`ta`) in the present or recent past, older or fallible personal memory (`ha`), and experiential or lived-through internal knowledge (`su`).
- indirect knowledge: inference from observable clues (`ci`) and tacit intuition or felt sense (`sa`).
- secondhand knowledge: informal spoken report or hearsay (`jo`) and trusted expertise, reputable sources, or established common knowledge (`yo`).

evidentials strictly encode information source; emotional reaction and evaluative skepticism are kept orthogonal in `c1.01` (`uv` affective and `ic` assessive).

# c1.06
## mood

cv markers that govern the illocutionary force of a clause or manage live conversational turn-taking. declarative statements are unmarked.

the eight elements split into two syntactic behaviors:

- clause-attaching speech acts: imperative and hortative (`zi`), prohibitive (`du`), polar yes/no question (`le`), and content question (`zo`, pairing with the interrogative pronoun `wa` in `c1.07`).
- standalone conversational interactionals: affirmative (`ji`), negative (`pu`), social greeting or closing (`ki`), and compliant acceptance (`to`).

# c1.07
## reference

cv nominal stems that track participants, anchor deixis, form questions, and link flat paratactic clauses without subclause nesting. because they function as nominal stems, they take `c1.01` case suffixes directly.

the eleven reference words cover four functions:

- personal pronouns: clusivity-marking first person exclusive (`mi`) and inclusive (`ze`), second person (`yu`), and proximate (`ke`) vs. obviate (`mo`) third-person tracking.
- variable and generic pronouns: interrogative (`wa`) and indefinite (`va`).
- deixis: a compositional demonstrative base (`lo`) that combines with lexical roots for proximal, distal, person-anchored, or multi-dimensional pointing.
- discourse pointers: anaphoric (`ju`), cataphoric (`mu`), and pre-anaphoric (`ka`), which point to adjacent clauses and take `c1.01` cases to express complex inter-clausal relations.

# c1.08
## quantity

cv numerals, quantifiers, and set-focus postfixes used for exact counting, mathematical expression, and scoping over sets. bare nouns carry general, number-neutral reference; `c1.08` markers attach only when count or set scope is needed.

the twelve elements cover three related systems:

- base-6 (senary) numerals: digits zero through five (`pa`, `we`, `tu`, `gi`, `so`, `lu`) and the positional base-6 multiplier operator (`fi`, representing `*6^`).
- set quantifiers: existential (`cu`), universal (`ce`), and distributive (`ru`).
- scalar and set focus: exclusive focus (`ca`, restricting to a sole referent) and additive focus (`g08.12`, adding a referent to an existing set or expectation).

# c1.09
## connector

binary cv infixes (`x-op-y`) placed between two stems to define how the right-hand stem (`y`) modifies, coordinates with, blends into, or possesses the left-hand head (`x`).

the eleven connectors divide into two groups:

- general compounding and coordination: the compound head-modifier divider (`pi`), symmetric coordination (`ge`), symmetric concept blending (`ve`), and the tail-specified general connector (`bi`), whose exact relation is defined by a single lexical root immediately following it (such as boolean logical connectives).
- possessive infixes: alienable ownership (`ri`), inalienable body-part, kinship, or inherent relation (`hu`), transient holding or use (`po`), social or contextual association (`vi`), abstract trait or state (`hi`), productive authorship (`ba`), and depictive or topic possession (`he`).

# c1.10
## degree

unary cv postfixes (`x-op`) attached inside stems to grade scalar dimensions or apply polarity and negation.

the nine elements form two symmetric subsystems:

- scalar grading: high (`la`), moderate (`ja`), low (`ti`), zero or privative absence (`wi`), excessive threshold (`go`), and insufficient threshold (`co`). this lets neutral dimension roots express poles symmetrically without lexical markedness, and powers comparative clauses when paired with `ak` (`c1.02`).
- polarity and negation: polar reversal or opposite (`si`), neutral logical negation (`te`), and alterity or other-than negation (`ho`).

# c1.11
## derivation

unary cv postfixes (`x-op`) attached inside stems to shift an entity's phase, configuration, participant focus, or metalinguistic framing.

the ten elements cover four derivational operations:

- phase and outcome: completive goal-reached outcome (`do`), inceptive beginning or nascent stage (`vu`), and terminative ending or residual stage (`de`).
- configuration: proximal portion or slice of a whole (`bu`) and coalescent collective or unified group (`yi`).
- participant conversion: converting a process stem to refer to its patient or target (`vo`) or its domain-neutral agent or doer (`be`).
- framing and essence: proper name or loanword marker (`ya`), representative essence for fictional, imagined, or depicted entities (`ra`), and explicit within-domain figurative metaphor (`ko`).

# c1.12
## domain

the eight unary cv postfixes (`x-op`) that shift a lexical stem out of its default home domain and into one of the eight ontological domains (`c2.01–c2.08`).

each suffix maps one-to-one onto a lexical domain: matter (`zu` -> `c2.01`), biology (`no` -> `c2.02`), motion (`re` -> `c2.03`), artifact (`ro` -> `c2.04`), sense (`ku` -> `c2.05`), mind (`me` -> `c2.06`), society (`ga` -> `c2.07`), and form (`pe` -> `c2.08`). attaching a domain postfix projects a root's core image-schema into a new conceptual realm, acting as a systematic metaphor and derivation engine. domain postfixes are omitted when a root is used in its default domain.

# c2.01
## matter

lexical roots for the non-living physical world, raw materials, substances, chemical elements, states of matter, geological terrain, celestial bodies, and weather phenomena (for example: stone, water, fire, air, metal, clay, rain, wind, star). targeted by the matter domain postfix `zu` (`g12.01`).

# c2.02
## biology

lexical roots for living organisms, flora, fauna, fungi, anatomy, organic tissues, life stages, health, and involuntary or homeostatic bodily functions (for example: plant, animal, seed, skin, bone, heart, eat, sleep, grow, death). targeted by the biology domain postfix `no` (`g12.02`).

# c2.03
## motion

lexical roots for kinetic actions, locomotion, posture, mechanical forces, physical interactions, transformations, and the transfer of objects through space (for example: move, arrive, stay, push, cut, break, hold, give, take, open, finish). targeted by the motion domain postfix `re` (`g12.03`).

# c2.04
## artifact

lexical roots for human-made objects, tools, vessels, clothing, shelter, built environments, prepared foods, media, currency, and technology (for example: tool, container, house, table, cloth, page, cord, bread, medicine, money). targeted by the artifact domain postfix `ro` (`g12.04`).

# c2.05
## sense

lexical roots for sensory perception, physical qualities, colors, luminance, sound, taste, smell, tactile textures, thermal states, and aesthetic or functional evaluations (for example: see, hear, sound, color, cold, sweet, clean, strong, good, bad). targeted by the sense domain postfix `ku` (`g12.05`).

# c2.06
## mind

lexical roots for cognition, emotion, mood, volition, memory, attention, imagination, beliefs, and internal psychological states (for example: feel, know, control, love, fear, play, sacred, think, decide, dream). targeted by the mind domain postfix `me` (`g12.06`).

# c2.07
## society

lexical roots for people, social roles, kinship, community, culture, communication, language, writing, art, custom, cooperation, conflict, and exchange (for example: person, parent, group, speak, name, symbol, custom, trade). targeted by the society domain postfix `ga` (`g12.07`).

# c2.08
## form

lexical roots for space, geometry, dimensions, relative position, boundaries, time, cycles, quantity, order, systems, patterns, and abstract relations (for example: size, time, center, front, back, edge, top, bottom, round, block, same, different, number). targeted by the form domain postfix `pe` (`g12.08`).
